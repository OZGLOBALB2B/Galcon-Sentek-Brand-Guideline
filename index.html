Introduction

This document will help you understand the core elements of Galcon-Sentek’s identity system.

It provides clear guidance on how to use the system consistently and serves as a source of inspiration for creating communications that are distinctive, coherent, and unmistakably Galcon-Sentek.

Together, these principles will help us continue building a strong global brand that reflects our commitment to innovation and meaningful impact.

2.7

Misuse

To maintain a consistent and recognizable brand identity, the logo must always be used according to the approved guidelines. Avoid altering, modifying, or recreating the logo in any way.

  • Do not distort the logoDo not stretch, compress, or change the proportions of the logo.
  • Do not rotate the logoAlways keep the logo in its original horizontal or approved orientation.
  • Do not change the logo colorsUse only the approved brand color combinations specified in these guidelines.
  • Do not add visual effectsAvoid shadows, glows, bevels, outlines, transparency, or other decorative effects.
  • Do not alter the typographyDo not replace, recreate, or modify the logo’s typeface, letterforms, or spacing.
  • Do not crop the logoThe complete logo must remain visible unless a separate cropped symbol has been specifically approved.
  • Do not change the order of the logo elementsAlways maintain the approved order and arrangement. Do not switch, reorder, or reposition the symbol, wordmark, or tagline.

4.0

Colors

4.1

Overview

The brand colors are intentionally vibrant and defined primarily in RGB to achieve their full visual impact across digital environments.

Since print systems such as CMYK and Pantone have a more limited color gamut, exact reproduction is not always possible. Printed applications may therefore appear less saturated, more muted and visually flatter than their digital equivalents.

5.0

Imagery

5.1

Overview

Galcon Sentek photography presents the people, environments and technology behind smarter irrigation. Images should feel authentic, confident and grounded in real working situations, whether in agriculture, public gardens or professional landscaping.

People should look natural and approachable, with a diverse mix of ages, genders and backgrounds. They are shown actively working, inspecting crops, maintaining irrigation systems or interacting with Galcon Sentek technology. Clothing should be practical and understated, using colors that complement the brand palette.

Natural light, open skies and rich green landscapes create a fresh and optimistic atmosphere. Warm morning or late afternoon light is preferred. Use a combination of wide environmental views and focused close ups to show both the scale of the setting and the precision of the work.

Technology should always appear as part of the real environment and clearly connected to the task at hand. The overall impression should communicate expertise, reliability and a close connection between people, technology and nature.

7.0

Graphic Layout System

7.1

Overview

The Galcon Sentek graphic layout system creates a consistent visual language across all brand touchpoints by combining our core visual assets: the logo-inspired shapes, authentic photography, product storytelling, and the brand color palette.

The system is designed to provide flexibility while maintaining a recognizable and cohesive identity. Layouts can adapt to different communication needs, from emotional brand storytelling to product-focused communication and technical content, while always preserving clarity, balance, and a strong connection between technology and agriculture.

Together, these three layout approaches create a flexible visual system that enables Galcon Sentek to communicate consistently across different channels.

7.2 · Approach 1

Logo-Inspired Graphic Overlay

The brand’s visual language is expressed through the use of the logo-derived shape as a dynamic graphic element placed over imagery.

The shape acts as a visual connection between the brand identity and the story being told, creating depth and recognition while allowing the photography to remain the main storytelling element.

Guidelines

  • Use the graphic element as an overlay, not as a dominant visual.
  • Maintain transparency or soft fades to create a natural integration with photography.
  • Ensure the image remains clear and impactful.
  • Use this approach primarily for emotional storytelling, campaigns, and brand communications.

Best suited for

Brand campaigns, banners, social media, exhibition graphics, hero visuals.

7.3 · Approach 2

Shape-Based Image Integration

The logo-inspired shape becomes an active container for visual content, allowing images, products, or people to be integrated directly into the graphic language.

This approach creates a stronger connection between the brand symbol and the content, transforming the visual identity into a storytelling tool.

Guidelines

  • Use the shape as a frame or cut-out for key visual elements.
  • Highlight people, products, or relevant agricultural environments.
  • Keep the composition clean and allow enough white space around the graphic form.
  • Combine with clear typography hierarchy for maximum impact.

Best suited for

Product communication, solution pages, presentations, posters, and key messages.

7.4 · Approach 3

Modular Bento Grid Layout

A flexible modular grid system designed to combine multiple content elements in a structured and visually engaging way.

The Bento layout allows the integration of product images, environmental photography, icons, key messages, and brand colors while maintaining clarity and consistency.

Guidelines

  • Use rounded modules inspired by the brand’s visual language.
  • Combine different content types while maintaining a clear hierarchy.
  • Apply the brand color palette to create emphasis and differentiation.
  • Balance informational elements with visual storytelling.

Best suited for

Product presentations, brochures, digital assets, sales materials, and technical communications.

10.1

Brand Balance

The tagline already holds the two forces the brand has to keep at once. Wisely is what we prove. Together is how we show up. Lean too far to the first and we sound like a spec sheet; too far to the second and we sound like everyone else.

Proof

Wisely

A grower commits a whole season to each irrigation decision and cannot afford to guess.

Decades of proven performance and agronomic knowledge, stated exactly. We show the reading, the reason and the result.

Wisely

Presence

Global strength with a local voice. We stay close, say what we will do, and succeed when our customers do.

10.2

Tone of Voice

Four attributes, two for each side of the balance. Every line we write should be recognisable as at least one of them, and should never break any of them.

Show examples for

Precise

Leans to Wisely

We name the crop, the depth, the unit and the result. Exact language lets a grower act on what we say without a second call. When we don't have the number, we say so instead of rounding up.

SpecificMeasuredExact

Examples for the grower

See moisture, temperature and salinity through the root zone, depth by depth.
Irrigate when the root zone needs it, not when the calendar says so.

Examples for the dealer

Every kit is listed with what's in the box, what it connects to and what it needs on site.

Examples for the integrator

Inputs, outputs and protocols are on page one of every spec sheet.

We say

  • Measures soil moisture, temperature and salinity at each depth.
  • Setup takes one visit. Here are the steps.

We don’t say

  • State-of-the-art sensing technology.
  • Dramatically improves yields.

Agronomic

Leans to Wisely

We start from the plant and the soil, then get to the technology. We explain what a reading means for this week's irrigation, in the words growers use with each other. Technical terms appear when they help, with a plain explanation beside them.

Examples for the grower

Examples for the dealer

Start the conversation with the crop. The controller comes second.

Examples for the integrator

Built around how a fertigation shift really runs, from the first valve to the last flush.

  • What this graph means for tomorrow's irrigation.

We don’t say

  • Unlock the power of your data.

We write as the person who will pick up the phone. We say "we" and "you", name the local team, and talk about the customer's season before our product. Friendly, never chummy: a grower's time comes first.

Promises & Perceptions

What each audience can hold us to, and the thoughts we want them to have after working with us.

Perceptions we want to build

  • It performs the same way season after season.
  • The system looks after itself, so I can look after the farm.
  • They stay close long after the sale.

Dealer

We make you the trusted advisor, with a solution that performs consistently and is easy to sell and stand behind.

Perceptions we want to build

  • My customers come back because it works.
  • I can explain the value in the grower's own terms.

  • I can start a customer small and grow the system with them.
